## The Move
### After-Tax Yield Rankings at 4.90%: Treasuries Just Beat JEPQ

Face-value yield rankings are almost always wrong for high-bracket investors. Here's the actual after-tax math for a 32% federal marginal bracket investor — the kind earning $200K–$375K as a married filer in 2026 — across four common income-generating positions.

**Assumptions:**
- Federal marginal rate: 32%
- State marginal rate: 5% (approximate national average for income-tax states)
- Long-Term Capital Gains (LTCG) / qualified dividend federal rate: 15% (applicable at this income level)
- Net Investment Income Tax (NIIT): 3.8% applies at this income level on investment income above the $250K married threshold — included for HYSA and JEPQ where most realistic; excluded from Treasury (state-exempt status is the more relevant adjustment there)
- State tax on qualified dividends: 5% (most states don't distinguish)

---

**1. 10-Year Treasury — 4.90% coupon yield**

Treasury interest is federally taxable, state-exempt (31 U.S.C. § 3124).

- Federal tax: 4.90% × 32% = 1.568%
- State tax: $0 (exempt)
- After-tax yield: **4.90% − 1.568% = 3.332%**

---

**2. Top High-Yield Savings Account (HYSA) — ~4.10% Annual Percentage Yield (APY)**

Fully ordinary income at both federal and state level. Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation (FDIC)-insured cash, so no credit risk — but maximum tax friction.

- Federal tax: 4.10% × 32% = 1.312%
- State tax: 4.10% × 5% = 0.205%
- NIIT: applies if MAGI clears $250K married threshold; 4.10% × 3.8% = 0.156%
- After-tax yield (with NIIT): **4.10% − 1.312% − 0.205% − 0.156% = 2.427%**
- After-tax yield (without NIIT, under threshold): **4.10% − 1.312% − 0.205% = 2.583%**

Using the NIIT-inclusive figure as the realistic case for most readers at this bracket: **2.43%**.

---

**3. Schwab US Dividend Equity ETF (SCHD) — ~3.5% trailing yield, qualified dividends**

SCHD's distributions are predominantly qualified dividends (historically 95%+). Expense ratio: 0.06% — essentially irrelevant to this calculation.

- Qualified federal rate: 15%
- State rate: 5%
- NIIT: 3.8% (applies to dividend income above MAGI threshold)
- Blended tax on qualified dividends: 15% + 5% + 3.8% = 23.8%
- After-tax yield: 3.50% × (1 − 0.238) = 3.50% × 0.762 = **2.667%**

---

**4. JPMorgan Nasdaq Equity Premium Income Exchange-Traded Fund (JEPQ) — ~9.0% trailing yield, mostly ordinary income**

JEPQ generates income primarily through Equity-Linked Notes tied to its options overlay. The Internal Revenue Service (IRS) classifies this as ordinary income — not qualified dividends — meaning it's taxed at marginal rates. Historically, 85–90% of JEPQ distributions are ordinary; assume 87% ordinary / 13% qualified for this calculation. Expense ratio: 0.35%.

- Ordinary income portion: 9.0% × 87% = 7.83%
  - Federal + state + NIIT: 32% + 5% + 3.8% = 40.8%
  - After-tax: 7.83% × (1 − 0.408) = **4.631%**
- Qualified portion: 9.0% × 13% = 1.17%
  - Tax: 15% + 5% + 3.8% = 23.8%
  - After-tax: 1.17% × (1 − 0.238) = **0.892%**
- Combined after-tax yield: 4.631% + 0.892% = **5.523%**
- Less expense ratio drag (approximate): −0.35%
- **Net after-tax yield: ~5.17%**

---

**The ranking:**

| Vehicle | Gross Yield | After-Tax Yield | Rank |
|---|---|---|---|
| JEPQ | 9.0% | ~5.17% | 1 |
| 10-Year Treasury | 4.90% | 3.33% | 2 |
| SCHD | 3.50% | 2.67% | 3 |
| HYSA (top rate) | 4.10% | 2.43% | 4 |

JEPQ still wins on after-tax yield — but the spread over Treasuries has compressed materially. At the 10-year's 2023 trough near 3.80%, JEPQ's after-tax advantage over Treasuries was roughly 220–230 basis points. At 4.90%, that spread has narrowed to approximately 184 basis points. The HYSA, once a reasonable placeholder for cash waiting to be deployed, is now the clear loser in this bracket — its after-tax yield trails the Treasury by 90 basis points on a fully risk-adjusted basis, with no duration premium, no state exemption, and full NIIT exposure.

The number that actually surprises: SCHD's modest 3.5% gross yield, via qualified dividend treatment, almost matches the HYSA's 4.10% gross yield after tax. For taxable accounts where you're already above the NIIT threshold, every new dollar parked in a high-yield savings account is working harder for the bank than for you.

One critical caveat on JEPQ: the after-tax yield advantage is real, but the gross yield isn't free income — it's largely return of volatility premium, with capped upside on the underlying Nasdaq exposure. In a ripping equity market, SCHD or a straight index position will outperform total return. The after-tax income ranking above is exactly that: an income ranking, not a total-return one.
