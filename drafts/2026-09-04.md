## The Move
### The $1.2M Cost of Retiring 100% Equity at 55

Sequence-of-returns risk is most lethal in the first five years of retirement. Not because later downturns don't matter — they do — but because early losses compound against you *while* you're withdrawing. The math is asymmetric: a 22% loss in year one on a $1.5M portfolio removes $330,000 that never gets the chance to recover. Every subsequent dollar of growth operates on a permanently smaller base.

Here's what that actually costs over a 35-year retirement (age 55 to 90) under three scenarios, starting balance $1.5M, 4% initial withdrawal rate ($60,000/year), inflation-adjusted annually at the current Core Consumer Price Index (CPI) of 2.5%, and 7% nominal returns in all subsequent years.

**Scenario A — Baseline: +7% nominal in year one**

Year 1 start: $1,500,000. Return: +7% = $1,605,000. Withdrawal: $60,000. End of year 1: $1,545,000.

For a 60/40 portfolio averaging 7% nominal over 35 years with 2.5% annual spending increases, the terminal balance at age 90 approximates $4.1M in nominal dollars. (This is a standard annuity-formula result: $1,545,000 growing at 7% for 34 years while withdrawals escalate at 2.5%. Net effective growth rate ≈ 4.37% real. Terminal value ≈ $1,545,000 × (1.07)^34 minus the present value of the growing withdrawal stream — running the numbers: withdrawal stream PV over 35 years at 7% discount with 2.5% growth ≈ $1,170,000 at retirement. Terminal ≈ $1,500,000 × (1.07)^35 − $60,000 × [(1.07^35 − 1.025^35)/(0.07 − 0.025)] ≈ $16.37M − $12.27M ≈ **$4.1M**.) Portfolio survives comfortably.

**Scenario B — Mild shock: −8% in year one**

Year 1 start: $1,500,000. Return: −8% = $1,380,000. Withdrawal: $60,000. End of year 1: $1,320,000.

That $225,000 deficit versus the baseline compounds at 7% for 34 years: $225,000 × (1.07)^34 = $225,000 × 10.68 ≈ **$2.4M** of terminal-value destruction. Projected terminal balance: approximately $1.7M. Portfolio survives, but thinly — the final decade relies on sub-3% real returns to avoid ruin.

**Scenario C — Sharp drawdown: −22% in year one**

Year 1 start: $1,500,000. Return: −22% = $1,170,000. Withdrawal: $60,000. End of year 1: $1,110,000.

The year-one deficit versus baseline is $435,000. Compounded at 7% for 34 years: $435,000 × 10.68 ≈ **$4.6M** of terminal-value destruction. Projected terminal balance: approximately −$500,000 (portfolio depleted around age 84). The 4% rule fails by six years.

**The gap between Scenario A and Scenario C: approximately $4.6M in terminal value — or, more practically, the difference between a funded retirement and one that runs out at 84.**

**What a bond tent or cash buffer actually does**

A bond tent allocates 20–30% to intermediate Treasuries or Treasury Inflation-Protected Securities (TIPS) in the 5 years before and after retirement, then glides back to equity. In Scenario C's drawdown year, that 30% bond allocation (earning ~4.5% on 10-year Treasuries at current yields) limits the portfolio loss to roughly −13% instead of −22% on the full portfolio — a $135,000 difference on $1.5M. That $135,000 compounded for 34 years at 7% = **$1.44M** in preserved terminal wealth.

Alternatively, a 2-year cash buffer ($120,000 in a high-yield savings account at ~4%) lets you avoid selling equity in year one of a drawdown entirely. Portfolio stays at $1,500,000 through the trough. If the equity market recovers to prior highs within 18–24 months (median S&P 500 bear-market recovery: 14 months historically), the early withdrawal is funded from cash, and the equity portfolio is untouched. Against Scenario C, avoiding that first-year sale at depressed prices is worth the full $4.6M gap compounded.

**The 100% equity position costs you the option value of not selling low.** At 4.79% on 10-year Treasuries, the bond tent costs nearly nothing in expected returns. The 0.03%–0.05% expense ratio on iShares Core U.S. Aggregate Bond Exchange-Traded Fund (ETF) (AGG) or Vanguard Intermediate-Term Treasury ETF (VGIT) is trivially small. You are not being compensated for sequence risk in the early retirement window — you're just absorbing it.

**The actionable framing:** at age 52–53, begin building the tent. By 55, hold 2–3 years of spending in short-term Treasuries or cash, and another 15–20% in intermediate bonds. Rebalance back toward equity starting at age 60. The 35-year portfolio doesn't need to be 100% equity to generate wealth; it needs to survive the first five years intact.
