## The Move
### The $500K State-Tax Arbitrage: Full Math, No Handwaving

The most underdiscussed lever for a dual-income household at $500K Adjusted Gross Income (AGI) isn't asset allocation or tax-loss harvesting. It's ZIP code.

**Step 1: Annual Tax Differential**

California's top marginal state income tax bracket (13.3%) applies above $1M for joint filers; at $500K AGI, the blended effective state rate sits around 9.3% on the marginal dollars above roughly $68K (the 9.3% bracket threshold under California Revenue and Taxation Code §17041). New York state similarly hits 9.65% above $323K for joint filers, with New York City layering another 3.876% on top — a combined city+state effective rate approaching 10.9% for NYC residents.

Texas, Florida, Washington, Nevada, Wyoming, South Dakota, and Tennessee impose zero state income tax on wages.

Applying a blended California effective rate of approximately 8.5% to $500K AGI yields **$42,500 in annual state tax**. For a New York City household, the combined effective rate is closer to 9.5%, producing **$47,500 annually**. Call the midpoint **$45,000/year** as the annual tax savings from relocating to a zero-income-tax state.

**Step 2: Compounded Terminal Value**

If that $45,000 annual savings is invested at a 7% real return (consistent with long-run US equity market real returns), the terminal value over 10 years using a future-value-of-annuity formula:

FV = PMT × [(1 + r)^n − 1] / r  
FV = $45,000 × [(1.07)^10 − 1] / 0.07  
FV = $45,000 × [1.9672 − 1] / 0.07  
FV = $45,000 × 13.816  
**FV ≈ $621,700**

That's the terminal-value differential — over $620K in real after-tax wealth over a decade — before netting out any cost-of-living adjustment.

**Step 3: Net the Housing Cost Differential**

This is where most analyses quit too early. California and New York housing costs are materially higher, but the gap is not uniform. A $2M San Francisco home vs. a comparable $800K home in Austin represents a $1.2M capital differential — but in a 7.03% mortgage environment, the carrying-cost difference on $1.2M of additional debt is roughly $84,000/year in mortgage payments alone, which swamps the tax savings entirely if you're buying in peak-CA on a mortgage.

However: renters and remote workers who own no California property, or who sell California real estate and redeploy capital, face a very different calculation. For a household that was renting in San Francisco at $5,500/month and relocates to Austin at $3,200/month, the housing delta is **+$27,600/year** of savings, stacking on top of the tax savings. Combined: ~$72,600/year in freed cash flow.

For a household that already owns in California, the calculus includes Proposition 13 property tax benefits (artificially low assessed values that reset on sale), which can run $15,000–$25,000/year in foregone property tax savings — partially offsetting the income tax gain.

**Step 4: The Often-Overlooked Offsets**

Zero-income-tax states recover revenue elsewhere. Washington state has no income tax but imposes a 6.5% state sales tax plus local rates averaging 2–3%, totaling 8.5–9% at retail — one of the highest effective sales tax burdens nationally. Texas has no income tax but property taxes average 1.6–2.2% of assessed value annually (vs. California's Prop 13-capped rates often below 0.7% on long-held properties). A $700K Texas home generates $11,200–$15,400/year in property taxes; a $700K California home assessed at 0.75% generates $5,250. That $6,000–$10,000/year offset is real but still leaves substantial net savings.

**Step 5: The Break-Even**

Capture just 50% of the $45,000 annual tax savings net of housing and local tax offsets — call it $22,500/year invested at 7% real for 10 years:

FV = $22,500 × 13.816 = **$310,850**

That's the floor-case terminal value improvement — assuming the move captures only half the theoretical savings and you account for higher property and sales taxes. Three hundred thousand dollars of additional terminal wealth in exchange for a change of address is positive expected value for nearly any Financial Independence, Retire Early (FIRE) timeline.

The move makes the most sense for: remote workers with no geographic revenue dependency, households renting (not owning) in high-cost states, and anyone within 5–7 years of a target Financial Independence (FI) number where compounding velocity matters most. It makes the least sense for: California property owners with a low Prop 13 basis, NYC households with significant city-only deductions, or anyone whose income is materially tied to a specific metro's client base.

One number to keep in your head: at $500K AGI, every additional year of California residence costs roughly **$621,700 in 10-year terminal wealth** at the theoretical maximum. Even at 50% efficiency, that's a $310K decision masquerading as a lifestyle preference.
